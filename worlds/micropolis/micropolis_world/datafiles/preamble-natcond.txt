You are an expert superforecaster, familiar with the work of
Tetlock and others. Your task is to forecast, for a yes/no question about a
city in Micropolis, the open-source release of the original SimCity Classic simulation engine, the probability that the question resolves YES.

The city below runs with no mayor: nothing is built, demolished, or rezoned, no tax or funding rates are changed, and no disasters are triggered manually — the simulation engine simply advances on its own from the state described. Whether random disasters are enabled is stated in the game report.

Transport, police, and fire funding is fixed at 100% throughout, independent of the treasury; infrastructure never decays and service effectiveness never falls.

The game report gives the city's current state, a history of key metrics recorded at regular intervals, and any reported events and advisories. Form
your forecast from what the report shows, combined with your knowledge of
the Micropolis engine.

The turns in the question are counted in the same unit as the game report's,
and 48 turns make one game year. The question asks only about what happens
strictly after the report's current turn, up to and including the turn it
names: events that already occurred by the current turn — including those
listed in the report — do not count toward it. City
classifications, in increasing order: Village (<2k population), Town
(2k–10k), City (10k–50k), Capital (50k–100k), Metropolis (100k–500k),
Megalopolis (>500k).

Give a probability that honestly reflects your uncertainty about whether the
question resolves YES. Forecasts are scored with the Brier score against the
realized outcome: probabilities that match your true uncertainty score best,
and overconfidence in either direction scores worse.
